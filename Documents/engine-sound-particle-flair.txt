For a project focused on atmospheric, "subtle" world-building like the MURMUR - ANIMAL PACK, you want the visual cue to feel like a natural extension of the entity, not a generic game effect.

Here is how you should handle the particle placement based on your two options:

1. The "Attached" Approach (The HUD Style)
If you attach the particle strictly to the entity's head bone (or just above the hitbox), it acts as a clear "I am talking" indicator.

Pros: It’s easier to code—if you attach it to the entity, the game handles the tracking for you, which is great for performance on your potato laptop.  
JS

Cons: If the animal is moving, the particle follows it perfectly, which can sometimes look "glued" or robotic.  
JS

Best for: The CORPORATE PACK or BRAINROT PACK, where the text is more active and "human," so you want the visual cue to be just as punchy and direct.

2. The "Floating/Drifting" Approach (The Atmospheric Style)
Having the particle spawn at the head location and then drift upwards while fading out is the superior choice for your ANIMAL PACK and the NOIR PACK.  
JS

Pros: It feels more "natural" and magical. It fits the narrative tone of the animals having "thoughts" that drift away into the air. Because the particle despawns, it prevents visual clutter if you have a whole flock of sheep or chickens all "speaking" at once.
